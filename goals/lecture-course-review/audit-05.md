## Depth still available

Grounded material in the eight source notes that the lecture does **not** yet use. All of it is quotable as-is.

**`notes/sparql-1-1.md` — the security and determinism warnings.** Nothing in the lecture hints that a query language has an attack surface: "Queries using `FROM`, `FROM NAMED`, or `GRAPH` may dereference IRIs, consuming network, disk, or CPU resources; the specification also warns about denial-of-service, local `file:` access, firewall-indirection attacks, extension-specific risks, and visually confusable Unicode IRIs." Also unused: "Pattern matches initially produce solutions in no specific order, so deterministic presentation requires `ORDER BY`", and the whole solution-shaping set — "Assignment, inline `VALUES`, aggregation, grouping, subqueries, expressions, and sequence modifiers such as `ORDER BY`, `DISTINCT`, `OFFSET`, and `LIMIT`". And the federation boundary: "federated-query behavior is defined in a separate SPARQL 1.1 Recommendation" — directly relevant to the decentralisation thread.

**`notes/shacl-shapes-constraint-language.md` — three unused capabilities.** SHACL is not only constraints: "It supports built-in constraints, extension through SPARQL or JavaScript, and **SHACL Rules for inferring new statements**." That last one complicates the clean OWL-infers/SHACL-validates split the lecture draws, and is worth the complication. Also unused, and a real trap: "When a property shape is included by a node shape, the property shape's own targets are ignored, so placement changes targeting behavior." And the report is meant to be spoken to humans: "Use the report's severity and messages to explain failures or suggest fixes."

**`notes/rdf-schema-rdfs-1-1.md` — the two pitfalls, both unused.** "Domains and ranges do not directly express restrictions local to one class, and multiple declarations mean membership in all declared classes." That is the single most common RDFS beginner error and the lecture does not warn about it. Also: "the specification says RDF Semantics is authoritative if the two documents disagree" — a nice concrete instance of the stack's layered-authority design.

**`notes/owl-2-web-ontology-language.md` — the syntax family and the soundness caveat.** Unused: "OWL/XML, Functional Syntax, Manchester Syntax, and Turtle serve other processing or readability needs" — Manchester Syntax in particular is what a listener will meet in Protégé.
