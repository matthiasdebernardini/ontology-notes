## Cut

1. **The Semantic Arts rebuttal argument — 118 words, wholly fabricated.** This is the defect the prior reviewer flagged.
   > "The reply's argument, in essence: Shirky picks examples where formal inference is silly, and concludes that formal inference is silly. But nobody proposes to derive social truths by syllogism. The proposal is to derive narrow, well-defined consequences within a bounded domain where the premises are curated by people who know the domain. If your ontology says every antibiotic in this class contraindicates with that drug, and the patient record says this patient is on that drug, the inference is neither profound nor controversial. It's a check that a human might miss at three in the morning, and it fires correctly a hundred thousand times a year."

   Reason: McComb's actual reply makes none of these moves. Its argument is that Shirky's own article is a syllogism, a traffic-light analogy for "consistently pretty good is good enough", and a failed attempt to formalise Shirky's soap-bubble doggerel. There is no drug-interaction example, no "bounded domain", no volume claim, anywhere in `crit-semanticarts-reply.md` or in the note. Replaced with what McComb does say, including his concession.

2. **The follow-on reconciliation built on that fabrication.**
   > "I think both sides are right about different things… The reply is right that inference over curated, bounded, expert-maintained categories is quietly load-bearing in several industries. Notice that the dividing line is the same one Shirky's own preconditions draw. The two positions agree more than either admits."

   Reason: it adjudicates an exchange that did not happen, and it leans on the non-existent "preconditions".

3. **"Read the essay carefully and it yields a set of preconditions, and they make a genuinely useful checklist."**
   Reason: Shirky's three/five preconditions do not exist. The note is explicit: "Shirky offers not a test but what he calls 'a partial list of characteristics'… Inverting the list into a go/no-go screen for a project is my framing, not his."

4. **"Genomics passes. Museum records pass. Financial instruments pass. Chemical compounds pass. … User-generated media fails. And a great many enterprise data projects fail three or four of them while believing they pass all five. Run your project against those five conditions honestly and you'll know whether you're building a Gene Ontology or a Yahoo directory."**
   Reason: none of these are Shirky's examples. His worked positives are the periodic table and DSM-IV; his named negative is the Web. The "five conditions" count is the same fabrication. The enterprise-projects sentence is unsourced narrator opinion dressed as a finding.

5. **"the same year as the Scientific American article and aimed squarely at it."** — see Corrected #1; the "aimed squarely at it" half is cut outright, not softened.

6. **"the technology stopped targeting the general public around 2008."**
   Reason: no source in the four notes contains 2008 or any such date. The retrospective's periodisation gives phases, not a public/enterprise cutover year.

7. **"he worked on RDF specifications as a teenager"** (Swartz).
   Reason: not in `semantic-web-retrospective.md` or its capture, which identify him only as "the famous programmer and another digital rights activist".

8. **The whole "fourth criticism I'd add… the cost of being wrong slowly" section (~230 words).**
   > "A database schema that's wrong causes an outage… by the time somebody notices, the wrong distinction is in four systems and two published datasets and a regulator's filing… That's an argument for competency questions with real expected answers, checked in the build."

   Reason: unsourced, and structurally destructive — it announces a fourth critique immediately before a closing whose whole point is "three critiques, three layers", and its conclusion duplicates lecture 7's claim to retain. Cut for grounding and for the ENDINGS requirement.

9. **The Aaron Swartz block (~330 words) — cut for structure, not for grounding.**
   Reason: it was correctly sourced to `semantic-web-retrospective.md` after the teenager clause was removed, but it is not a beat in the lecture-8 plan, it belongs to the lecture-5 subject matter, and it was the spliced second-pass material that produced the "two more voices… they attack from inside" seam. Removing it restores the three-critic spine and shortens the file. Reinstate deliberately from "Depth still available" if a later pass wants a fourth voice.

10. **"None of them applies to signal you observe."**
    Reason: overclaim stated as source. Search engine optimisation, which the same lecture cites, games observed signal. Replaced with a hedged narrator gloss.
