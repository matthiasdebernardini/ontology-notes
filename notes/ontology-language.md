# Ontology language

**What it is**
Ontology languages are formal, usually declarative languages for constructing ontologies and encoding domain knowledge. They often include reasoning rules and are commonly based on first-order logic or description logic.

**Key concepts**
The page classifies languages both by syntax—traditional, markup, controlled natural language, and open-vocabulary natural language—and by logical structure—frame-based, description-logic-based, and first-order-logic-based. Examples include Common Logic and KIF, RDF/RDFS and OWL, and Attempto Controlled English.

**How you'd use it**
Use the classifications as a map for choosing a representation style: markup-oriented interchange, controlled-language authoring, frame modeling, description-logic classification, or first-order predicates.
